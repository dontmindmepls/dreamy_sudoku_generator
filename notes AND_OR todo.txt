## EXPLICIT THOUGHT PROCESS REGARDING HOW TO POPULATE 9x9 GRID ##

I don't actually know.

Huh.

Right now, my thought is to go row-by-row. Basically, what I would do is that I would start by generating 
a new pre-rotated (or transmutated, whatever) 3x3. That would serve as the 3x3 in the first slot. However,
I would only populate [0][0], [0][1], and [0][2] with the same [0][0], [0][1], and [0][2] as in the 3x3.
So on and so forth until [0][8] by calling shuffle_grid_rows(). However, there are still the bottom two rows
full of ints to work with. So what I guess I would do is create six temporary integer arrays of three, and 
with those I would store the ints in the bottom two rows of each generation. I would have six full arrays 
total and then I could just copy and paste the results of those arrays into the bottom two rows of the next
three grids of the 9x9. Then I go down to the [1][0] grid of the column and call shuffle_grid_columns() and 
then rinse and repeat the strategy until all is filled.

I think it is a bit inefficient though. There is probably a faster way to do this. 

Going down a different path, what I could do is just generate the 3x3 grid the first time around and then fill
from [0][0] to [2][2] in the 9x9, then call shuffle_grid_rows() and do it again for the next square. Rinse and
repeat and call shuffle_grid_columns() where needed until the whole grid is full. This approach would go instead
of row-by-row grid-by-grid. 

I am leaning more towards this option that I have just written it, but it is likely also inefficient.

As I am still learning, however, I do not know of any more efficient way to be able to do these things, so either
these or some other approach I think up will have to do. We shall see.
